Living Data is a catalog of interactive data visualizations. Each one explores an open data dataset and gives it a form entirely its own.

## The approach

There is no chart template here, reused from one dataset to the next. Each visualization is a piece of its own: its form is chosen for what the data has to show, whether an animated map, a circular calendar, small multiples or a swarm of points around a globe. The aim is to spark curiosity, then let everyone handle the representation and make up their own mind.

## The data

All data comes from public sources: public agencies, scientific institutions, conservation organizations, collaborative projects. Each page credits its sources with their publisher, their license and the date they were retrieved: open data changes over time, and this date records the version actually used.

The data is prepared and frozen when the site is built, unless the page says otherwise. When a dataset has known limits (partial coverage, documentation bias, observations rather than a census), the page says so.

When the source licenses allow it, the files prepared for a visualization can be downloaded from its page. Reusing them remains subject to those licenses.

## Sharing a view

A visualization's address follows what you do with it: filters, a paused year, a zoomed-in area of a map. Copying that address is enough to share exactly the view on screen.

## How it's made

The site is static, generated with [Astro](https://astro.build) and hosted on GitHub Pages. The visualizations are coded one by one, mostly with [D3](https://d3js.org) and Canvas. The source code is public on [GitHub](https://github.com/cheubes/dataviz.heubes.io).

No audience tracking, no cookies: only your language preference is stored, in your browser. Fonts and icons are served by the site itself: displaying a page contacts no third-party service.
